    FAQ

    What is the extracellular matrix and why is everyone talking about it?
    Everyone knows the body is made of cells. Cells live in an environment called the extracellular matrix, which is studied by molecular biology. Scientists around the world have been actively researching its properties and aging since the second half of the last century, and many studies are published every year. This year, for example, there was a study by the well-known Clarke laboratory at a partner institute of the University of Cambridge.

    Until recently, the extracellular matrix was considered a relatively inert scaffold that stabilizes the physical structure of tissues. It is now clear that it plays a significant role. The extracellular matrix is present in all body tissues, so disruptions in how it works lead to connective tissue diseases, premature aging and cell death. There are many diseases associated with connective tissue disorders, and they can be severe and reduce patients' quality of life.

    The extracellular matrix relies on three key components. Collagen maintains the framework and structure of tissues, hyaluronic acid fills the spaces and provides hydration. Elastin gives tissues significant elasticity and firmness. It is named after this property and is also called the "youth protein". With age, the extracellular matrix degrades and changes for the worse, which leads to various age-related diseases. Problems with the extracellular matrix are an important cause of aging. The first visible signs of aging are often linked to irreversible changes in the extracellular matrix. >>Video.
