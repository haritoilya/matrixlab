A few important facts about elastin

1.
Elastin is the protein that drives skin regeneration; it is also responsible for the strength, firmness and stretchability of tissues. Together with collagen and some other fibrillar proteins, elastin is found in the intercellular substance (matrix) of connective tissue, forming a three-dimensional network of protein fibers.
2.
This network is not only important for the mechanical strength of tissue but also provides contacts between cells, forms migration paths along which cells can move, and separates different cells and tissues from each other (for example, it enables gliding in joints).
3.
Elastin performs important functions in organs that are constantly stretched and compressed, such as arteries, the aorta, lungs, skin, ligaments, tendons and various sphincters.
4.
Elastin and collagen fibers help organs return to their original size and shape after stretching, for example when skin is pinched and during other natural age-related processes.
