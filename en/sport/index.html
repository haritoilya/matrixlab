    Joint and ligament recovery

    A ligament is connective tissue that connects bones to other bones at joints. This connective tissue contains both collagen and elastin, which allows the ligament to stretch to a certain extent. Ligament damage, often the result of a sports injury, can seriously restrict proper knee movement (for example, being unable to turn or twist the leg).

    Elastin supports healthy joint function and strengthens ligaments

    It is known that with age, elastin function declines in the skin and blood vessels; likewise, the function and amount of elastin in ligaments also decreases, which is believed to cause knee pain.

    Therefore, substances that activate cells of the knee's cruciate ligament are expected to be effective in strengthening ligaments and preventing injuries.

    Fish elastin has been confirmed to stimulate ligament cell proliferation, elastin mRNA expression and type I/III collagen mRNA expression.

    Fish elastin improves ligament condition by activating ligament cell production.

    In addition, an inhibitory effect on osteoblast-like differentiation was confirmed by measuring ALP (alkaline phosphatase) activity, suggesting that ossification of ligaments may be suppressed.
